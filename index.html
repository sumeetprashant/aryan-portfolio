MIT / AI privacy research

A private chat. An unexpected audience.

What happens beyond the chat window? I led a research team tracing network traffic from commercial AI tools to find out.

The study found that 9–36% of captured requests from four platforms went to third-party domains, raising questions about AI use in healthcare.

Co-authored with Leo Anthony Celi (MIT) and Joseph Sakran (Johns Hopkins).

Prognosis & Harvard / Disease forecasting

A head start can change what’s next.

Dengue doesn’t wait for a dashboard. Public-health teams need time to act.

Two related chapters in my work used satellite imagery and machine learning to help turn environmental signals into earlier warning.

Kiwi Senior Health / Product & machine learning

A little watch. A lot riding on it.

A fall detector has to know the difference between a wrist moving and a person needing help.

I owned the model, the product, and the messy path between them. Sixty-nine model configurations later, we had something ready to run on the watch.

The person behind the projects

I go where I’m needed.

That’s usually where I learn the most.

At Kiwi, that meant writing the roadmap, hiring engineers, talking to suppliers, and personally testing and packing the watches we shipped.

It also meant sitting with elderly users and helping them get comfortable with a new device. A better model is only useful if someone wants to wear it.

In July 2026, I stepped into interim co-CEO responsibilities alongside the founder. The scope grew. The instinct stayed the same: understand the details, take responsibility, and keep things moving.
