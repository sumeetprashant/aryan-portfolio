Kiwi Senior Health, Machine learning

A fall detector that took 69 tries to get right

You met it at the top of this page. The real one was trained from scratch on 81.2 hours of labeled wrist data: 368 falls from 10 people. I tried transfer learning first. The pretrained features didn't survive our hardware, so I dropped it.

The winner is a bidirectional LSTM that fuses six orientation-invariant motion signals with 33 hand-built physics features, checked on people it had never seen.

The honest limit: every tester was a working-age adult, not the elderly people it's for. Strong on the test group, not yet proven for customers.

MIT, Published research

What your AI chatbot quietly tells a third party

I led a 10-person team that put commercial chatbots behind Wireshark and Burp Suite and counted where the traffic went. Between 9% and 36% of requests left for analytics and tracking domains.

For anyone typing health details into a chatbot, that is a HIPAA-sized gap. Co-authored with Prof. Leo Celi (MIT) and Dr. Joseph Sakran (Johns Hopkins).

Lockheed Martin, Sponsored capstone

A pendulum rig for 150 lb drones, on a $1,250 budget

How do you measure a drone's moment of inertia without a lab? Hang it from two wires, twist it, and time the swing. I led a six-person Penn State team that built it for Lockheed Martin engineers: aluminum frame, load cells, a gyroscope, embedded C on a Teensy 4.1.

Delivered on spec with $71.89 left in the budget.

An academic capstone with Lockheed sponsors, not Lockheed employment. A strong prototype, not a certified instrument.
